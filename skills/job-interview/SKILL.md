# Interview preparation

Read the exact job, history, submitted CV/answers and invitation. Establish time,
timezone, people, format, stage and what the user wants to practise. Research
current employer/interviewer facts when they change preparation.

Save a useful brief under `artifacts/<job-id>/interview.md`:

- the likely problem this hire should solve, with inference labeled
- probable evaluation criteria and questions, ordered by the user's risks
- factual stories mapping problem, personal action, result and relevance
- bridges for real gaps and practice tasks appropriate to this profession
- confirmed compensation, authorization, availability and competing timelines
- questions that help the candidate evaluate the team and working conditions
- exact logistics and promised next steps

Scale the depth to the interview and user's request. A finance case, portfolio
review, behavioral screen and coding interview need different practice. During
mock practice ask a question, hear the answer and give specific feedback; do
not write a flattering fictional transcript. Use candidate-voice for lines the
user will say. Mark hypothetical exercises separately from real achievements.

Afterward capture what actually happened, objections, questions and next step.
Update pipeline only from evidence. Keep reusable lessons with the process and
promote repeated lessons into campaign preferences when useful. An enthusiastic
conversation is not yet an offer; compare actual written terms against the
user's goals when they arrive.
